# Channel and Blog Research Policy

Finreels’ channel and blog research must support the same educational objective as the book library: a learner should be able to move from beginner to intermediate, advanced, and professional competence. Sources must therefore go beyond business commentary and include fundamentals, academic foundations, practical and technical skills, career development, industry knowledge, psychology and human behavior, communication, leadership, strategy, problem-solving, critical thinking, research, ethics, history, applications, case studies, entrepreneurship and finance where relevant, innovation, creativity, advanced specialization, and professional development.

Every channel and blog must be real, relevant to its assigned category, useful for learning, independently verifiable, and accessible without a paid subscription or paywall wherever the content is being recommended as free. The research should prefer universities, professional institutions, government agencies, established educational publishers, respected practitioners, and consistently maintained specialist publications. Topic fit must be judged against the category’s full knowledge structure, not only its commercial or business side.

Each existing category should contain 10 channels and 10 blogs after the update, and General should also contain 10 channels and 10 blogs. Existing entries must be audited for duplicates, broken links, weak relevance, and overlap. Additions should be category-specific and should not be copied across categories unless the source genuinely supports multiple learning pathways.

The channel-feed implementation should not assume that 15 videos is the complete channel inventory. It should retrieve as many available items as the source API or feed reliably exposes, paginate or follow continuation tokens where supported, deduplicate by video ID, retain publication metadata, and apply the user’s selected-category weighting only after the available source content has been collected. Where a provider imposes a hard limit, the app should document that limit and use the strongest supported alternative rather than pretending the first page is the full channel history.

Sources and retrieval methods must be documented in the repository. No fabricated channel IDs, blog URLs, video records, or claims of completeness are permitted.
